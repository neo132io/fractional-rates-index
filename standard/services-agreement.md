### 1.2 Engagement structure

Three structures cover most fractional executive work. Each fits a different problem, and choosing
the wrong one is the most common commercial failure in these engagements.

| Structure | Fits | Does not fit | Pricing unit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Retainer** | Ongoing leadership: owning the function, the roadmap, the team, the exec-level voice. Work is continuous and value compounds | A one-off decision with a defined end. A retainer for a bounded question overpays for availability | Per month, quoted **with** an hours or days commitment |
| **Project** | A bounded question with a definable "done": a strategy, a diagnostic, a portfolio assessment, an integration plan | Open-ended leadership, or any scope genuinely unknown at the outset | Fixed fee, often with a stated duration |
| **Day rate** | Intensive but intermittent: workshops, diligence, a strategy intensive, a controlled one-day-a-week cadence | Continuous leadership. Day-rate billing prices attention as a commodity and suppresses the between-days thinking leadership work depends on | Per day. The definition of a "day" varies and must be written down |

**Where the scope is genuinely unknown**, the defensible pattern is a short project to define scope,
then re-contract. A fixed fee against an undefined scope transfers all discovery risk to one party
and reliably produces a bad engagement.

**On comparability.** A monthly figure without an hours figure is not comparable to another monthly
figure. The index records providers publishing similar headline numbers against materially different
time commitments, which is why the fee field and the time-commitment field below are a single
commercial term recorded in two places.

## 6. Intellectual property

Written out in full, because the failure mode here is silence rather than disagreement.

The common pattern, and the one this standard recommends:

- **The client owns the deliverables.** Strategies, roadmaps, documents, research and artifacts
  produced for the client are the client's, typically assigned on payment.
- **The consultant retains their methods.** Frameworks, templates, know-how and general skill brought
  to the engagement remain the consultant's, with the client receiving a licence to use them
  internally as embedded in deliverables.

**Write this split down explicitly.** An agreement that assigns "all work product" without carving
out pre-existing methods can, read literally, assign the consultant's entire practice. That outcome
is almost never what either party intended, and it is discovered late.

## 10. Handover

An engagement that cannot be handed over has not succeeded.

Common practice: a defined handover period, documented decisions **and their rationale**, and a named
receiving owner. The rationale matters more than the decisions; a successor who inherits conclusions
without reasoning will re-litigate all of them.
