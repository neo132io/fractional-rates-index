# The Fractional Market's Favorite Numbers: A Reality Check
### Fractional Rates Index · Analysis · August 2026 · v1.0

Every article about the fractional executive market cites the same handful
of statistics. We traced each one to its origin using two independent
research passes (different AI research systems, different source trails),
then compared the genealogies. The short version: of the industry's four
favorite numbers, one is traceable but uncalculated, one is a disclosed
model, one has no primary source at all, and one appears to be a case-study
metric that escaped its context.

This document exists because we publish pricing data ourselves, and data is
only worth citing when its lineage survives inspection. We hold the
market's numbers to the standard we hold our own.

---

## Claim 1: "The fractional executive market is worth $5.7B, growing 14% a year"

**Verdict: traceable claimant, undisclosed calculation. Cite only as
"X estimates", never as a measurement.**

This is the most widely circulated market-size figure, and downstream it is
attributed to at least four different origins: an Over50Pros "market
benchmark", the FRAK Conference's State of Fractional report, a report by
the Canadian placement network Solace, and generic "industry research".
Several sites cite it with no attribution at all. One research pass traced
the earliest inspectable publication we could locate to Think Fractional's
"Global State of Fractional Consulting 2025", which states the figure directly
and describes a survey of 150+ consultants. We could not find a disclosed
calculation connecting a 150-person survey to a global dollar figure.

The attribution pattern is itself the finding. When one figure is credited to
four unrelated origins, the citation trail is no longer telling you where the
number came from, and a reader has no way to evaluate it.

## Claim 2: "The market is worth $9.4B (2025), heading to $24.7B by 2034"

**Verdict: the most methodologically disclosed figure in circulation, and
still a proprietary model, not a measurement.**

This figure traces cleanly to a single origin: Dataintelo's "Fractional
Executiveplace Market Research Report", using 2025 as the base year. Unlike
every other market-size claim, the publisher discloses a methodology:
mixed-method design, top-down and bottom-up estimation, secondary sources
including commercial databases, and primary interviews. The inputs and
math remain proprietary, and the firm publishes thousands of syndicated
reports across unrelated sectors, so precision should not be confused with
accuracy.

Note what the two claims together imply: the two dominant "market size"
numbers differ by roughly 65% for the same period, with no published
bridge between their scopes. There is currently no independently validated
size for this market. Anyone who tells you otherwise is quoting one of the
two numbers above.

## Claim 3: "120,000 fractional executives in the US, double the 60,000 of 2022"

**Verdict: no primary source located. Treat as unsourced unless one surfaces.**

This is the industry's favorite supply-side statistic, and it has appeared
in Forbes and Fast Company, which is precisely how it became
unquestionable. Neither research pass located a primary source
for the 60,000-to-120,000 series. It is most often attributed to the FRAK
Conference's 2024 State of Fractional report; FRAK's own official release
says something different: that more than 140,000 LinkedIn professionals
list "fractional" in their job title. The 60,000 baseline, the doubling,
and the US framing appear nowhere in a primary document; the population
definition, geography and endpoints mutate as the claim moves downstream.
Where a survey basis is referenced at all, it is a self-selected sample of
roughly 250 conference-adjacent professionals, which cannot size a
population.

A LinkedIn title count is a real, if crude, indicator of self-labelling. The
doubling series presented on top of it is a separate claim, and we could not
find a published basis for it.

## Claim 4: "Fractional CMO adoption grew 245% in two years"

**Verdict: we could not trace it to a source. Cite only with the qualification
that its origin is undocumented.**

The earliest fully inspectable, dated instance found attributes the figure
to "Geisheker & Associates research" (described elsewhere as a
survey of 500+ companies); neither research pass could locate that report,
dataset or methodology. A second provider attributes the identical figure to
its own proprietary analysis, which we were not able to inspect. Separately,
within the same niche, "245%" appears in vendor case studies as a social-media
growth result and as a demo-request increase. We are not able to say whether
these uses share an origin. What we can say is that the same figure is applied
to three different phenomena and that none of the three carries an inspectable
source.

## What this means for anyone citing fractional market data

1. **There is no validated market size.** Use ranges and name the
   estimator: "estimates range from $5.7B (Think Fractional, survey-based,
   calculation undisclosed) to $9.4B (Dataintelo, modeled)".
2. **The only defensible supply-side indicator** currently public is the
   LinkedIn job-title count (140,000+ per FRAK's release), which measures
   self-labeling, not full-time-equivalent supply.
3. **Adoption-growth percentages in this market have no published basis we
   could find.** Measuring growth in adoption requires a repeated-measure
   study, and we located none. Treat such percentages as unsourced.
4. **Published prices are the exception, not the rule.** In our verified
   dataset, 95 of 738 providers (12.9%) have a browser-confirmed published price, and no
   unconfirmed price remains in the dataset. Among CPO providers the confirmed rate is 5 of 106. A separate structured
   150-provider cross-role discovery pass found first-party published
   pricing for fewer than a third of providers, with CTO the most
   transparent role and CFO/CRO the least; our merged, unverified
   candidate queue (315 providers) is published in this repository for
   the verification runs ahead.

## Methodology of this analysis

Two independent deep-research passes (August 2026) traced each claim's
citation genealogy: identifying downstream citers, attributed origins,
and any disclosed methodology, then cross-compared. Where the passes
disagreed on provenance, both findings are reported above.

**What "we could not locate" means and does not mean.** Every statement here
describes the result of a documented search carried out in August 2026. It is a
statement about what those searches found, not an assertion that a source does
not exist, and not a statement about the conduct or good faith of any person or
organisation named. Publishers described here may hold methodologies, datasets
or reports that are not public, or that our searches did not reach.

**Corrections.** If you are named here and can point to a primary source, a
disclosed calculation, or anything else that corrects the record, open an issue
on this repository or write to us. Corrections are published with the version
that carries them, and this document is updated rather than defended. The provider
candidate queue from the discovery passes ships in this repository under
research/; the screening list from the contact-export pass and the full
citation-genealogy trails are held in the project archive and available
on request. This document will be updated if
primary sources surface.
